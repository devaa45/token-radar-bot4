How the values are worked out

The model is not picked by hand. Fourteen families are fitted on the protocols whose token already trades at a recorded market cap, and each is scored held-out twice: five-fold cross-validation (folds by an FNV-1a hash of the slug; the score is the median multiplicative error, so ×3 means half the held-out predictions land within a factor of three) and a walk-forward split (the earliest-tracked 70% trains, the latest 30% tests). The cross-validation winner, refitted on all the priced protocols, prices the board. Its cross-validation signed errors' middle half is the band on every row: honest width, not decoration.

Two things the table shows plainly. Feature tilts do not survive contact with held-out data: the age tilt and the full linear model test worse than the plain power law, and the walk-forward column catches the bucket model coasting on recent listings. What survives is an ensemble: the median of a power law, a winsorized-ratio power law and a TVL-bucket ratio. Every row's detail view shows that arithmetic — the three component values and their median — beside the band.

Implied value = 10 raised to the winner's predicted log10 market cap, from the protocol's own TVL (and, in the families that use them, its seven-day growth, its age and its chain spread). No protocol's name is an input anywhere.
